Good Dog's exercises read as lists. An exercise screen shows its preparation, steps, what to look for and what to do if it's hard as sections of text, and the owner has to scroll back and forth while holding a lead and a treat. Turn an exercise into a guided session the owner can follow one step at a time.

- A "Start guided session" button on the exercise screen opens the guided session.
- The session shows one thing at a time: first a short "get ready" card, then each step on its own, with the step's number and the total ("Step 2 of 5"). Large text and big Back and Next controls that are easy to hit while holding a lead.
- The existing session timer keeps running across the steps, and is visible on every card.
- After the last step, the session ends on the same outcome choice the exercise screen already has (easy, getting there, too difficult, plus the welfare concern option). Choosing one records the session exactly as recording from the exercise screen does today: same rules, same history entry, same skill progress.
- Leaving the session part way (closing it, or the app being terminated) records nothing. Reopening the exercise starts the guided session again from the start.
- "If it's difficult" is one tap away on every step card, without losing the place.

Keep the session's state and rules in Rust (which card is showing, moving next and back, what may be recorded and when); SwiftUI only presents it. Every new control gets an accessibility identifier named screen.element and a label a VoiceOver user understands. The guided session must work at the largest text size and in dark mode.

Add a requirement for it to requirements.toml (GD-6), with a Rust or Swift model test as its twin evidence and a UI test file in uitests/ as its device check.
